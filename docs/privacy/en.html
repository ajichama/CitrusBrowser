CitrusBrowser Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 28, 2026

This privacy policy explains how CitrusBrowser ("the app"), a web browser for Android, handles information.

  • Developer: ajichama
  • Contact: GitHub Issues. This is a public page, so please do not post personal information.

1. The developer does not collect your data

The app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting SDKs. The developer does not operate any server for the app, and the app does not send your data to the developer.

2. Information stored on your device

The following is stored on your device for the app's features and is not sent to the developer:

  • Browsing history, bookmarks, open tabs, speed dial and read-it-later pages
  • Search history (used to show suggestions)
  • Cookies, site data, saved form entries and other data stored by websites
  • Settings, user scripts, ad-filtering lists and settings
  • Downloaded files (in the location you choose)
  • App error logs (never sent)

You can delete this information from the app's settings and history screens, by clearing the app's data in Android settings, or by uninstalling the app. Private mode and per-site data clearing are also available.

3. When the app communicates with outside services

As a web browser, the app communicates with the websites you open. In addition, it communicates with outside services in the following cases. Information sent is handled under each recipient's privacy policy.

  1. Search suggestions: When you type in the search box, the text you type is sent to the suggestion provider you selected (Google, Microsoft Bing or DuckDuckGo; Google by default) to show suggestions. Set "Search suggestions" to "Past queries" or "None" to stop this.
  2. Search: When you search, your search terms are sent to the search engine you selected.
  3. Ad and tracker filter lists: When ad filtering is on, the app periodically downloads filter lists from their publishers (for example easylist.to, filters.adtidy.org, ublockorigin.github.io and raw.githubusercontent.com). No user data is sent, but as with any connection, the publisher receives your IP address and similar information. Lists you add are downloaded from their publishers.
  4. Page translation: When you use the translate action, the URL of the current page is sent to Google Translate.
  5. Reader view and icons: When you open reader view, or when the app fetches an icon for speed dial or a home-screen shortcut, the app connects again to the site you are viewing.
  6. Safe Browsing: Android System WebView (provided by Google), which displays pages, may communicate with Google to check for dangerous sites.
  7. Downloads: Files you choose are downloaded from their servers.

4. Device permissions

These permissions are used only for the purposes below. Location, camera and microphone are used only when a website asks and you allow it. You can revoke permissions at any time in Android settings.

  • Internet and network state: browsing, search, downloads and filter-list updates
  • Location: to give your location to a website (such as a map) that asks for it, when you allow it
  • Camera, microphone and audio settings: when a website (such as a video call) asks and you allow it
  • Notifications and foreground service: to show download progress and keep downloads running when you leave the screen
  • Request to install packages: to hand a downloaded app (APK) that you open to the Android installer
  • Local network (Android 17 and later): to open pages on devices in your home network when you choose this in settings
  • Storage (Android 9 and earlier only): to save downloads and backups
  • Other: keeping the screen awake, vibration, and adding shortcuts to the home screen

5. Android backup

If backup is enabled in Android settings, the app's data (such as bookmarks, history, and settings) may be backed up to your Google Account by Android. Login state (cookies), data stored by websites, open tabs, and caches are excluded from both backups and device-to-device transfers. This is a Google feature, and the developer cannot see the backed-up data. You can turn it off in Android settings.

6. Children

The app is not directed to children under 13.

7. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, this page will be updated with a new "Last updated" date. Major changes will also be announced in the GitHub release notes.

8. Open source

The app is open-source software under the Apache License 2.0, based on the open-source Yuzu Browser by Hazuki. Source code: https://github.com/ajichama/CitrusBrowser
